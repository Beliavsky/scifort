## Error metrics and limitations

Raw scaled error divides the maximum absolute difference by the larger
of maximum reference magnitude, maximum input magnitude, and minimum normal
binary64 value. Offset comparison additionally includes 1 and `abs(dln)`
in the scale because the defining condition locates a grid phase.

For bias span `abs(q)*(n-1)*abs(dln) > 8`, the checker also includes this
bound on amplification through the separate bias diagonals and FFTs:

```text
B = sqrt(n) * max(abs(u_effective))
    * max(abs(input)*exp(input_bias))
    * max(exp(output_bias))
```

`u_effective` is the forward multiplier or reciprocal conjugate inverse,
including the even Nyquist projection. The square-root factor converts
the Fourier operator's 2-norm bound to an infinity-norm bound. The
acceptance scale is the maximum of the raw scale and `B`, with bound
`3e-12`. Ordinary cases and high-precision cases with modest bias use
the raw scale directly. This checks rounding amplification without
mistaking a discrete inverse pair for proof of continuous-integral accuracy.

Results:

| Build | Maximum raw scaled error | Maximum error relative to the amplification bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Checked | `1.685e-9` | `2.662e-13` |
| Optimized | `1.685e-9` | `2.657e-13` |

The worst raw case is the inverse with `n=31, dln=0.7, mu=100,
bias=0.8` at its low-ringing offset, compared with the direct high-precision
sum. A separate forward comparison in the same sensitive regime measured
raw errors of `1.642e-9` for SciFort and `1.560e-9` for SciPy against the
high-precision sum. Wide bias can therefore reduce accuracy in both
implementations. See the [Hankel guide](../docs/hankel.md) for singularity,
range, and interpretation contracts.
